## 2. Write-up template

The article template keeps its rail, main column, and sticky sidebar, and adds figures. A write-up is prose in sections with these figure types, each numbered and captioned, each with a provenance line:

- **Capture**: an image or a short muted looping video recorded from the real app. Reduced motion pauses the video and shows controls. Videos carry a captions track describing the action.
- **Flow**: a diagram of the real pipeline or loop drawn from the code, as a vertical sequence of steps; a step may be a row of parallel nodes. No box appears that does not correspond to a module, table, or state in the repository.
- **Table**: a Markdown table of measured results, quoted from the project's published docs.

Figures reveal on scroll; under reduced motion they are simply present. Nothing is hidden without JavaScript.

**Provenance line examples.** "Recorded from the real app against a local database of made-up players." "Drawn from `scripts/` and `Docs/DB_SCHEMA.sql` in the repository." "From the public engineering docs, results for May 10 to Aug 13, 2026."

**What a write-up may not do.** Show a capture of anything holding real financial data (PFC has no captures). Name the employer, data vendors, or real firms behind Beacon (the repository's own sanitizer forbids it; the site follows the same rule). Describe OnAir as a way to watch broadcasts, or name stream sources; it ships none, and its demo runs against local fixture streams. Claim a result a project has not produced (BT's market-maker experiment is a plan; BJS has not shipped an app).

BJS is a native iOS trainer for players who want to get seriously better at blackjack: basic strategy drills, Hi-Lo counting, house-edge analysis, and a full card-counting simulation. It is an educational tool, not a casino, and its credibility rests on one thing: the numbers must be right. BJS is the project's name; the app is called Feltwise, and its App Store name is "Feltwise: Blackjack Trainer".

## Why it exists

Every other trainer I tried either taught generic strategy that was wrong for the table you were actually sitting at, or hid the math behind cartoons. BJS is rule-aware. You configure the exact rules of the casino you are playing (dealer hits or stands on soft 17, doubling after a split, resplitting aces, surrender, deck count, penetration) and strategy and edge calculations adjust to match.

## The engine

All of the math lives in a pure Swift package with no UI dependency, so it is tested in isolation and can be reused by any front end. Strategy is decoded from Wizard of Odds' published basic-strategy charts and matches them cell for cell across 48 rule combinations, so the play shown is the one for the configured table, not one generic chart. Randomness is injectable, so shoe shuffles are reproducible in tests.

The edge calculator looks the configured rules up in Wizard of Odds' house-edge table for basic strategy dealt with a cut card. Two rules the table doesn't offer are derived: a 2:1 payout exactly, from the 3:2 and 6:5 figures, and early surrender from Wizard of Odds' rule-variation figures. The breakdown starts from the 8-deck baseline game, 0.45% for stand on soft 17, double after split and 3:2, and changes one rule at a time in a fixed order, so each rule's step is a real difference between two table entries and the steps add up to the total. It replaced an earlier model that added a fixed delta per rule, which was off by up to 0.4 percentage points; 38 reference cases from the Wizard of Odds calculator now check it, each to within 0.00001.

## Feltwise Pro

Step 9 adds one paid tier. Free keeps the Learn and Test strategy modes for every rule set with the Why explanations, running-count drills of 10, 26 and 52 cards at half a second a group or slower, the card-values reference, the edge calculator, Progress, and one Shoe Sim shoe per install. Pro adds the Speed and Weak Spots modes, the full-shoe running count and faster paces, the whole true-count drill, and unlimited shoes. It is sold as a one-time Lifetime unlock or a yearly subscription with a one-week free trial, at launch prices of $39.99 and $24.99 a year set in App Store Connect; the app shows StoreKit's own price string and holds no price in code. There is no paywall at launch, during onboarding, in Progress or in the middle of a drill: Pro options stay visible with a mark and open the paywall only when tapped, and closing it puts you back in the same setup. The free shoe counts as used only once its report shows past the halfway point, and it is tracked with a local flag, so resetting progress does not give it back. If Pro lapses, the drills lock again and no history is lost. There are no ads and no third-party SDKs.

## Status

The original app layer was discarded on 23 September 2026 and rebuilt on the package in eight steps: engine completion, a design system, the strategy trainer, counting, the edge calculator, progress, shoe simulation, and launch prep. All eight are merged to main. The engine gained a round engine with splits, surrender and peek, property tests over a hundred thousand seeded rounds, and strategy decoded from Wizard of Odds; the Felt design system has tokens with tested contrast pairs, shared components and a debug catalogue; the strategy trainer, the counting drills, the edge calculator and progress work end to end. The shoe simulator deals a shoe at a chosen penetration, 60 to 85 per cent, self-paced or at a dealer's pace of 0.5 to 2 seconds a card, grades every decision, and stops to check the running and true count. Launch prep capped text at the AX3 accessibility size app-wide, ran Xcode's accessibility audit on every stop of a screen tour at that size, went through each area with VoiceOver, and added the app icon, App Store metadata and a privacy manifest that declares no tracking and no collected data. It was merged on 9 November 2026, the day the app was named Feltwise, and build 1.0 (1) went to TestFlight.

Feltwise Pro is Step 9, built on its own branch and not merged. Build 1.0 (2), which carries it, was uploaded on 11 November 2026 and is in TestFlight's internal group; the step is done when a sandbox purchase test on a device passes. On that branch there are 292 engine tests, 428 app tests and 26 UI tests, with no third-party dependencies. Nothing is on the App Store. Before it can be, the paid-apps agreement needs banking and tax details, the App Privacy questionnaire needs answering and the age rating confirming, and the free Lifetime offer set up for build 1 testers can only issue codes once the app is approved. The repository has been private since 9 November 2026.

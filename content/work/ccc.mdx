Clippers Command Center is a Clippers dashboard with statistical context: a live game view, a between-games home, players, the schedule and six seasons of history. Every stored insight carries the query that proves it, and every proof is re-run nightly.

## What it does

Five pages behind one top bar and a ⌘K palette that searches players, games and pages. Home is the between-games view. Live is the game view: scoreboard, quarter line scores, a dense box score, the score in the browser tab, and a sticky mini-scoreboard. Players has a page per player with a headshot, rolling averages, splits and a game log. Schedule labels back-to-backs, rest days, home stands and road trips. History covers every season in the database game by game, with a results strip and streaks per season, play-in and playoff games labeled and kept out of the records, and drill-through to every box score.

## Provable insights

The product rule is simple: **if a claim cannot be verified from stored data, it does not display.** Each insight row stores the parameterized SQL that supports it, the parameters it ran with, the rows that SQL actually returned when it was executed, and a stable identity key.

```text
proof_sql     parameterized SQL that supports the headline
proof_params  the ids and dates it ran with
proof_result  the rows proof_sql returned; empty means the insight is rejected
proof_hash    sha256(category | team | player | game | season | metric): identity, not proof
```

A generator whose proof comes back empty is dropped before the upsert. The identity key is a unique index, so re-running the engine updates an existing insight instead of duplicating it, and any insight a run does not re-emit is deactivated. Every night a verify step re-runs every active proof and fails the job if the stored rows no longer come back. Batch categories run in dependency order: scoring and shooting streaks, season milestones, rare events (the top 1% of every NBA player-game that season), opponent context, league comparisons, and year over year. Claims are scoped to one stats season, the latest with at least ten Clippers games, regular season only, for players who finished the season with the Clippers. Importance is a base score per category plus rarity and recency boosts, capped at 100, and the client rotates the top of the pool.

Live insights are different. They are pure functions over the latest live data and the rolling ten-game context, they are never written to the table, and they carry no stored proof. Two detectors: a scoring run of eight or more unanswered points in the last two minutes of play-by-play, and a clutch flag for the last five minutes of the fourth quarter or overtime with a margin of eight or less.

## Live without flicker

A game-night runner polls the NBA feed on a cadence set by the state of the game: every three seconds in live play, two in the clutch, eight during stoppages, thirty early in halftime. It fetches the box score only when a new play appears and writes one live-state row per game. The live endpoint is served through Vercel's CDN, so the origin answers about once every two seconds per region however many people are watching, and the live page follows the cadence the runner advertises while the site-wide top bar refreshes every thirty seconds. One poller makes about 3,000 requests to the NBA feed over a game.

The live page never calls the NBA feed itself. It reads the live-state document through that one endpoint, which reports one of three states: no active game, data delayed, or live. When the newest data is too old to trust, the page shows last-known numbers under a "Data delayed" banner instead of silently serving them as current.

This pipeline replaced a fixed twelve-second loop in September 2026 and has not yet run during a real game. The first will be the season opener on 21 October.

## Pipeline

Separate scripts, one job each: sync the schedule and attach NBA game ids, sync the odds, run the live game night, sync every NBA game's box scores league-wide (the last three days nightly, or a whole season on demand), finalize games, compute stats incrementally (possessions, pace, offensive and defensive rating, shooting efficiency, five- and ten-game rolling windows), generate insights, verify insights, and backfill. Every write is an idempotent upsert, so any script can be re-run. GitHub Actions drives them: the schedule daily, odds every six hours, the game-night runner, and a post-game job that syncs, finalizes, computes, generates and verifies in that order. No queue, no workflow engine. Cron authentication fails closed with a constant-time compare, failures return real error codes instead of a quiet 200, and CI runs typecheck, lint and 331 Vitest cases against a Postgres service. The whole thing runs on free tiers: Vercel Hobby, Neon Free and GitHub Actions.

## What it is not

No accounts and no personalization. Odds are for display and insight context, not betting. The live pipeline is unproven until it runs through a real game. And the engine can only prove what is in the database, so a batch claim is scoped to one stats season and the one before it, regular season only.
